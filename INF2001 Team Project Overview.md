# INF2001 Introduction to Software Engineering
## Team Project Overview

This document details the overview details of your team project.

### Overview

Each team will work on a different project with different context and requirements. Your team will need to apply your learnings from the module and produce quality deliverables by following the timeline.

The project descriptions can be found in the folder under: “LMS->INF2001->Team Project->Project Description”.

### Stakeholder Engagement

Each team needs to conduct engagement sessions with the “Stakeholders” and the Stakeholders can be team members from your own team or team members from other teams or from real world. Please refer to Appendix A on instructions on how to engage your Stakeholders.

### Assessment

There are 2 milestones for your team project for the 1st and 2nd half of the project respectively. The requirements and rubrics for each milestone can be found in Appendix B (1st half) and Appendix C (2nd half).

There will be peer evaluation for your team project. The grade for each individual member will be weighted based on the contribution and peer evaluation.

### AI Usage

While it is not prohibited to use AI for the team project, all teams must use AI tools critically and ensure the learning outcomes are achieved.

In case AI tools are used, please include a section at the beginning of the Milestone Report and articulate (i) how and where AI tools are used; and (ii) what changes are made on top of AI outputs.

Please note all teams will be asked to elaborate on randoms parts during presentations.

## Appendix A - Instructions to Engage Stakeholders for Team Project

### As the Project Team

Here’s a quick summary of the minimal duty required for each individual team.

1. You may find team members in your own group or from another group or from the real industry to be the Stakeholder Representatives for your team project.
2. Every team should have engage at least 5 Stakeholder Representatives with at most 2 representatives from your own team.
3. Try to arrange in-person meetings with the Stakeholder Representatives as early as possible from Week 2 of the trimester. It is the team’s responsibility to initiate the meeting and keep your Stakeholder Representatives familiarized with the project context and requirements in advance such that they’re able to provide valuable inputs.

### If you’re approached by other teams to act as their Stakeholder Representative

1. Familiarise yourself with the project description and requirements in advance. In the project description, there are some pointers for the stakeholders to consider.
2. Consider possible gaps that the team may question. You will want to think of an answer or process so that you can give the project team a uniform answer.
3. Please provide all your best assistance to play the role of the Stakeholder Representatives. Each student cannot be acting as Stakeholder Representatives for more than two teams (including your own team) and cannot be engaged in Stakeholder meetings for the same project.
4. Please keep your engagement discussions with other teams confidentially.

### During the Engagement Meeting with Stakeholders

1. The students may use various requirement gathering technique during the Stakeholder’s meeting, and the Stakeholder Representatives should work with them during the meeting.
2. The Stakeholder Representative is allowed to add requirements as they deem fit. However, the requirements in the project description are the minimum each project team must deliver.
3. Record the attendance of the meeting and include it in your Milestone Report.

### Deadlines

1. Arrangement of the Stakeholders meeting can only start from Week 2 of the trimester.
2. All engagements (in-person, email, virtually) must end by Week 6 of the trimester.

## Appendix B. Requirements and Rubrics for Milestone 1 (1st half)

For Milestone 1, each team needs to submit a Milestone 1 Report and make a group presentation by Week 6.

### B.1 Requirements for Milestone 1 Report (20%)

#### B.1.1 Content (15%)

The Milestone 1 Report should include a chapter for each of the items as below.

- Introduction
  - Cover page
  - Team members and their contributions
  - Plagiarism declarations
  - Elaborations on AI usage and declarations with changes made on the AI outputs (if available)
- Requirement Engineering (4%)
  - Requirement Elicitation Process
  - Software Requirement Specifications
- Use Cases (5%)
  - Formal use cases
  - Use case diagrams
  - Activity diagrams
- Object-oriented Analysis (4%)
  - Identified key classes with diagrams and relationships among the identified classes
  - Sequence diagrams for the use cases with the identified classes
- Project Management (2%)
  - Work Breakdown Structure
  - Project timeline

#### B.1.2 Presentation (5%)

##### B.1.2.1 Formatting (2%)

- Professionally structured report, clear language, good formatting, references where needed.
- All artifacts align (e.g., requirements ↔ use cases ↔ diagrams).
- Terminology consistent throughout.
- No contradictions.

##### B.1.2.2 Group Presentation (3%)

- The presentation slides should cover the key deliverables for each of the items covered in the Milestone 1 Report.
- The team can choose the number of presenting members and it is not compulsory to have the whole team to make the presentation.
- Logistics information, e.g., presentation date and time, will be shared later.

### B.2 Submissions

- Each team only needs to submit one zipped file including the Milestone 1 Report and the presentation slides.
- The zipped file should be named as: `INF2001-M1-Team X.zip`. For example, Team P1-1 should name the zipped file as: `INF2001-M1-P1-1.zip`.
- The deadline for submission is: 11:59PM, 9 Oct 2026 (Friday).

A penalty of 20% per day for each deliverable will be imposed for late submission unless extension has been granted prior to the submission date. Request for extension will be granted on a case-by-case basis. Any work submitted more than 4 days after the submission date will not be accepted and no mark will be awarded.

### B.3 Peer Evaluation

All students need to complete their peer evaluation by 11:59PM, 9 Oct 2026 (Friday). If not, there will be a 10% penalty for your own peer evaluation mark and the contribution credits will be evenly distributed among the other team members.

### B.4 Rubrics

#### Component Diagram (3%)

| Component | Criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Component Diagram** | - The component diagram should be complete, logically correct, and use appropriate UML 2.5 notation.<br>- The component diagram must be consistent with the final class diagram and adequately support the identified user requirements/use cases.<br>- The diagram must be accompanied by a clear description of the responsibilities of the major components and their interactions/interfaces.<br>- Dependencies and interfaces between components should be clearly and correctly represented. |

#### Detailed Design (5%)

| Component                 | Criteria                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| :------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Architectural Pattern** | - An appropriate architectural pattern is selected and correctly applied to the system.<br>- A clear rationale is provided explaining why the selected architectural pattern is suitable for the system and its requirements. The discussion should identify the main responsibilities of the architectural elements/layers.<br>- A supporting diagram illustrating the application of the selected architectural pattern must be provided.<br>- The supporting diagram must be consistent with the final class and component diagrams and clearly illustrate the relevant interactions or dependencies between system elements. |
| **Design pattern**        | - ONE (1) suitable design pattern that addresses an identified design problem in the system is selected and supported by a clear rationale. The explanation should describe the design problem addressed by the pattern and how the pattern improves the proposed design.<br>- A relevant excerpt of the class diagram must be provided to illustrate how the selected design pattern is implemented.<br>- The diagram must be consistent with the final class diagram and clearly identify the roles/classes participating in the design pattern.                                                                               |

#### Testing (4%)

| Component | Criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Blackbox Testing** | - A clear description of the selected function/method and its expected behaviour is provided.<br>- The description must be supported by a complete decision table that includes causes, effect, and combination, together with a reduced table (if it applies).<br>- Test cases must be systematically derived from the final decision table, with clear inputs, expected outputs, and traceability between each rule and its corresponding test case(s).<br>- The selected function/method should contain sufficient conditional logic to demonstrate meaningful application of decision-table testing. |
| **Whitebox Testing** | - A clear description of the selected method is provided. The selected method should contain sufficient control-flow complexity to allow meaningful white-box testing.<br>- The analysis must include the method's pseudocode, a corresponding Control Flow Graph (CFG), and a correct calculation of cyclomatic complexity.<br>- The selected method should result in a CFG containing at least eight nodes and two decision points. |

#### Wireframe (3%)

| Component | Criteria |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Wireframe** | - The final wireframe must provide adequate coverage of the identified user requirements and use cases.<br>- Wireframe shows good design, functionality and user centered focus.<br>- Each relevant wireframe screenshot should be accompanied by a concise description and clearly mapped to the corresponding use case(s). |

| Component              | Criteria                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
