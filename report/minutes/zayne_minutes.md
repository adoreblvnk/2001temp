**Zayne:** Hey Chloe, thanks so much for taking time right after your piano lesson to talk with me today.

**Chloe:** No problem, Zayne! Happy to help. What’s this project about?

**Zayne:** Our team at SIT is designing a new workload allocation and scheduling system for the school. Even though the primary users are the managers and teachers, students are obviously at the very heart of the school. We want to understand what the scheduling experience feels like from your perspective—how you find out about your lesson times, what happens when you need to change a timing, and where things get frustrating.

**Chloe:** Oh wow, that’s great. Believe me, I have some thoughts on rescheduling!

**Zayne:** Let’s start with that! What instrument do you play, and how long have you been learning here?

**Chloe:** I play piano, and I’ve been with the school for about two years now. Piano is by far the most popular instrument here—there are tons of us. Fortunately, every single studio has an upright or grand piano in it, so getting a room has never really been a problem for me personally.

**Zayne:** That’s an interesting point about the rooms. What’s your current routine for booking lessons? How often do you book?

**Chloe:** Well, I don’t book fresh lessons every single week. When I first enrolled, the school assigned me a recurring weekly 30-minute slot—every Thursday at 4:30 PM. So for the most part, it’s a regular weekly commitment. The real headache only starts when my school timetable changes, or when I have exams or project submissions and I need to move or reschedule a lesson.

**Zayne:** Walk me through what happens right now when you need to adjust or reschedule your 30-minute lesson.

**Chloe:** Right now, it’s honestly super slow and manual. I have to write an email to the school office or send a WhatsApp message to Marcus, the manager. I’ll say, "Hey Marcus, I have a school lab test next Thursday at 4:30 PM, can I move my lesson to Friday after 5:00 PM?" Then I just have to wait. Marcus has to check his spreadsheet, message my teacher to see if she’s free, and then email me back. That usually takes a full day, sometimes two days. And the worst part is, by the time he replies, another student might have taken the Friday slot, and we have to start the email chain all over again!

**Zayne:** That back-and-forth delay is such a classic scheduling bottleneck. If you could design how it works in the new system, what would you prefer?

**Chloe:** Ideally, I’d love to just log in on my phone, see which slots my teacher actually has open that week, and click to request a reschedule. Even if the manager or my teacher still needs to review and hit "approve", just being able to see real-time open slots and track the request status—like `Pending Manager Review` or `Confirmed`—would save so much anxiety.

**Zayne:** How do you currently find out about your confirmed lesson timings each week or month?

**Chloe:** Usually, they tell me when I first enrol, and if nothing changes, I just assume it stays the same. But occasionally, if my teacher has a masterclass or takes leave, the school sends a broadcast email or message. It’s not always super clear ahead of time, and there have been times when I wasn’t 100% sure if my lesson was on until the day before. I really wish there was a clear, centralized calendar where I can just check my weekly slot whenever I want.

**Zayne:** Do you typically get your first choice of timeslot when you enrol or when the month’s schedule is set?

**Chloe:** Not always. Because I’m a student, I have secondary school classes during the day. So I can only do weekday evenings—after 5:00 PM—or weekends. But guess what? Every other student in Singapore wants those exact same hours! So those prime slots fill up almost immediately. I usually end up with something workable, but it’s often my second or third preference. If the school had a transparent view of availability, or could show me alternative open slots when my preferred time is packed, that would be amazing.

**Zayne:** Earlier you mentioned you never struggle to find a studio for piano. Do you think that applies to other instruments as well?

**Chloe:** Definitely not for drums! My good friend at school learns drums here. He complains all the time because only Studio 5 has a drum kit. If someone else is in Studio 5, he literally cannot have his lesson, even if his drum teacher is standing right there in the hallway. For piano or violin, they can use any room, but the drum students are always competing for that one room.

**Zayne:** That confirms exactly what the IT admin and manager told us about Studio 5 being an acoustic and equipment bottleneck. What device do you usually use to check school messages or timetables?

**Chloe:** My phone, without question. I’m almost never on a laptop unless I’m doing homework. If I’m checking my lesson time or checking whether Marcus approved a reschedule, I’m pulling my phone out on the bus or during recess. So if there's any student-facing interface or email notification, it has to look good and be easy to read on mobile screens.

**Zayne:** Would you rather be assigned a timeslot automatically, or be shown your teacher’s open availability and pick the slot yourself?

**Chloe:** I definitely prefer picking from my teacher's open slots! Being assigned a time is fine when it happens to fit, but school schedules are so unpredictable these days. If I can see three open slots that my teacher is genuinely free for, and I can pick the 5:30 PM slot that lets me finish my cca, that would make my life so much easier.

**Zayne:** That makes complete sense. While our first milestone is focused primarily on the manager and teacher back-office platform, capturing your needs helps us ensure the manager’s reschedule workflow can instantly locate those open slots and handle parent/student requests with zero delay.

**Chloe:** That would already be a huge improvement! If Marcus can see open slots in five seconds instead of digging through spreadsheets for two days, we all win.

**Zayne:** Exactly right. Thanks so much for your time and honesty, Chloe. Really appreciate your help!

**Chloe:** You’re welcome, Zayne! Good luck with the project!

### 3. Key Discussion Points & Operational Findings

#### 3.1 Recurring Lesson Allocations & Enrolment Workflow
- Students are predominantly enrolled in **recurring weekly individual 30-minute sessions** tied to a fixed weekday/weekend slot.
- Primary demand peak is concentrated in **weekday evenings (after 17:00)** and **weekend full-day blocks (08:00 – 21:00)**.
- While recurring slots remain stable, friction spikes sharply during academic examination periods or co-curricular activity (CCA) commitments requiring temporary schedule adjustments.

#### 3.2 Current Rescheduling Pain Points & Communication Lag
- Reschedule requests currently traverse an asynchronous email/WhatsApp communication loop involving the student, parent, shift manager, and instructor.
- **Turnaround Overhead:** Processing a single reschedule request requires between **24 to 48 hours**, frequently leading to lost slots where candidate open openings are filled before confirmation.
- Students require real-time visibility into open candidate slots and an instant status tracking mechanism (`Pending Confirmation`, `Rescheduled`, `Cancelled`).

#### 3.3 Facility Bottleneck Perceptions
- **Piano, Violin, and Trumpet:** Studio availability is rarely perceived as a constraint due to piano provision in all 5 studios. The primary bottleneck is teacher availability during peak hours.
- **Acoustic Drums:** Corroborates severe facility dependency. Studio 5 is recognized by students as a strict physical bottleneck that restricts drum scheduling flexibility.

#### 3.4 System Scope & Architectural Alignment
- While full student self-service direct booking is designated as a Phase 2 extension in the Project Baseline Agreement (Section 7), the administrative system must provide managers with a dedicated **Lesson Reschedule & Enquiry Processing Module** (`FR-14`, `UC-15`).
- This module allows managers to immediately query qualified teacher availability and unbooked studio slots in real time during phone or front-desk enquiries.
